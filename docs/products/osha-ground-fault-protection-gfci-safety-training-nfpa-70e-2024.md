### Overview

## OSHA Ground Fault Protection / GFCI Safety Training (NFPA 70E 2024)

A comprehensive safety training program designed to help workers understand ground fault hazards and properly use Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs) to prevent electrical injuries and fatalities.

## Course Overview

The **OSHA Ground Fault Protection / GFCI Safety Training (NFPA 70E 2024)** course provides essential knowledge for identifying electrical hazards and implementing proper ground fault protection practices in the workplace. This training is aligned with **OSHA electrical safety requirements** and the **NFPA 70E 2024 Standard for Electrical Safety in the Workplace**. Participants will learn how GFCI devices help prevent electrical shock, electrocution, and equipment damage by quickly interrupting ground fault currents.

## Who Should Enroll?

- Construction workers and electricians working with electrical equipment
- Maintenance personnel responsible for electrical systems and tools
- Safety officers and supervisors overseeing electrical safety programs
- Employees who operate power tools or equipment in environments with electrical hazards

## What You'll Learn

The **OSHA Ground Fault Protection / GFCI Safety Training** course includes key modules covering:

- Understanding ground faults and the electrical hazards they create
- The purpose and function of Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs)
- OSHA requirements for ground fault protection in construction and general industry
- Proper inspection and testing of GFCI devices
- Safe work practices for using electrical tools and equipment
- Methods for preventing electrical shock and arc hazards
- Best practices for implementing workplace electrical safety programs

## Regulatory Requirement

OSHA requires employers to protect workers from electrical hazards by implementing ground fault protection measures where electrical tools and equipment are used. This training supports compliance with **OSHA electrical safety regulations**, including **29 CFR 1926.404(b)(1)** for construction and applicable safety practices outlined in **NFPA 70E 2024**.

## Renewal and Validity

While OSHA does not specify a fixed renewal period for this training, employers are encouraged to provide periodic refresher training to ensure workers remain familiar with electrical safety practices and updated standards such as **NFPA 70E 2024**. Regular training helps maintain compliance and strengthens workplace safety programs.

 **Note:** Proper use of Ground Fault Circuit Interrupters (GFCIs) is one of the most effective methods to prevent electrical shock incidents. Employers should ensure that workers understand how to inspect, test, and use GFCI protection when working with electrical tools and equipment.

### Course Objective

By the end, you'll be equipped to:

- Identify key OSHA and CAL/OSHA electrical standards for construction and general industry.
- Explain the concept of a grounding path and its importance in protecting workers from shock and electrocution.
- Differentiate between electrical system (circuit) grounding and equipment grounding.
- Identify the essential characteristics of an effective grounding path: permanent, continuous, sufficient current-carrying capacity, and low impedance.
- Describe how a fault current travels through the grounding path to activate protective devices.
- Recognize common improper grounding hazards and their potential consequences.
- Identify equipment, tools, and metal components that must be grounded.
- Explain how GFCIs detect leakage current and interrupt power to prevent ground-fault injuries.
- Differentiate between types of GFCIs: circuit-breaker type, receptacle type, portable type, permanently mounted type, and cord-connected type.
- Identify Class A and Class B GFCIs and their trip specifications.
- Explain the testing procedures and frequency for GFCIs to ensure proper functionality.
- Recognize situations where GFCI protection is required according to OSHA, NEC, and NFPA 70E standards.
- Identify conditions where GFCI protection may be inadequate, such as damaged cords or bypassed devices.
- Recognize hazards associated with wet conditions and the role of GFCIs in prevention.
- Apply best practices for grounding and GFCI use in wet or damp environments.
- Demonstrate proper use of a GFCI in temporary power setups and cord-and-plug connected equipment.
- Conduct Job Hazard Analyses (JHAs) to identify and mitigate electrical hazards.
- Implement an Assured Equipment Grounding Conductor Program (AEGCP).
- Apply proper documentation, reporting, and training procedures for electrical safety.
- Demonstrate proper use of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for electrical safety.
