### Overview

## OSHA Hand & Power Tools Safety Training

Our **OSHA Hand and Power Tools Safety Training** course helps organizations maintain a safer workplace and meet OSHA compliance requirements. The program focuses on ergonomic hazards, correct tool usage, inspection and maintenance routines, and the dos and don’ts of safe tool operation for both hand and powered tools.

## Why This Course Matters

Working around tools is one of the most common sources of workplace injuries. Proper training reduces injuries, downtime, and regulatory risk by ensuring workers understand the hazards and controls associated with tools and equipment.

- Aligns employer practices with OSHA’s regulation for hand and power tools (29 CFR 1926 Subpart I).
- Reduces incidents caused by incorrect tool use, poor maintenance, or ergonomic strain.
- Protects workers, reduces lost-time incidents, and lowers potential compliance penalties.

## Who Should Take This Course?

This training is suitable for a broad cross-section of workers and supervisors including:

- Construction workers and laborers using a wide range of hand and power tools.
- Maintenance staff and technicians who repair and maintain equipment.
- Supervisors and foremen responsible for enforcing safe work practices.
- Warehouse and manufacturing employees involved in material handling and tooling tasks.
- Employers and safety managers seeking OSHA-compliant tool training programs.

## What This Course Covers

The course delivers practical and regulatory guidance across key areas:

- Overview of OSHA requirements (29 CFR 1926 Subpart I) and employer responsibilities.
- Types of hand and power tools and appropriate, safe applications.
- Pre-use inspections, routine maintenance, and identifying wear, damage, or malfunction.
- Ergonomic hazards and techniques to reduce strain and repetitive-motion injuries.
- Selection and correct use of PPE for specific tools and tasks.
- Electrical safety, guarding, and protecting against flying debris and pinch points.
- Safe storage, transport, and lockout/tagout considerations for portable equipment.
- Emergency response, accident reporting, and immediate corrective actions.
- Practical checklists, inspection templates, and maintenance schedules to implement on-site.

Whether you are new to construction sites or an experienced worker at equipment-heavy worksites, this training will strengthen hazard recognition and give you practical controls to keep teams safe.

 **OSHA Compliance:** Completion supports employer documentation of training and helps satisfy OSHA training expectations under Subpart I.

### Course Objective

By the end of the OSHA Hand and Power Tools Safety Training course, you’ll be able to:

- Understand the importance of hand and power tool safety in preventing workplace injuries and ensuring OSHA compliance.
- Recognize common hazards associated with hand and power tools, including electrical, mechanical, and impact risks.
- Identify proper handling techniques and best practices for using different types of tools safely.
- Learn about necessary personal protective equipment (PPE) required for safe tool operation.
- Understand the importance of regular inspection, maintenance, and proper storage of tools to prevent malfunctions and accidents.
- Recognize OSHA requirements for machine guarding, grounding, and other essential safety measures.
- Develop strategies for preventing injuries related to improper tool use, such as cuts, burns, shocks, and flying debris.
- Implement effective training programs for employees to enhance workplace safety and reduce tool-related incidents.
