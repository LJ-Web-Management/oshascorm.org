## OSHA Emergency Action Plans (EAP) Training

Our OSHA Emergency Action Plans (EAP) Training equips learners with the knowledge to create, implement, and maintain a customized Emergency Action Plan that meets all OSHA requirements. A strong EAP is essential for workplace preparedness and plays a critical role in protecting employees during emergencies.

Emergency preparedness is a core element of any safe work environment. A well-developed emergency action plan helps employers reduce the risk of injuries, fatalities, and property damage during unexpected events. By establishing clear procedures and regularly training staff, organizations can foster a safer, more confident, and better-prepared workforce.

A robust EAP outlines step-by-step actions for responding to different types of emergencies, including fires, explosions, severe weather, medical emergencies, chemical spills, active threats, and bomb threats. Through this course, learners gain an understanding of essential EAP components—such as evacuation strategies, alarm systems, reporting protocols, assembly points, and communication methods—to ensure swift and coordinated responses.

### Course Objective

## Learning Outcomes — Emergency Action Plan (EAP) Course

After completing this course, learners will be able to:

- **Explain the purpose and primary components of an Emergency Action Plan (EAP)** — why EAPs matter and what they must include.
- **Understand the key steps in developing and implementing an effective EAP** — from hazard identification to training and drills.
- **Explain regulatory requirements and OSHA standards governing EAPs**, including the responsibilities of employers and employees.
- **Identify OSHA regulations on exit route design, construction, capacity, and maintenance** to ensure safe egress paths.
- **Differentiate between exit access, exit, and exit discharge** — the distinct components of a compliant exit route.
- **Identify options for medical attention and first-aid training** available within the organization and how to integrate them into the EAP.
- **Recognize the significance of regular review and updates of the EAP** to keep plans current with workplace changes and lessons learned.
- **Describe the key elements of fire prevention plans** that reduce ignition sources and limit fire spread.
- **Recognize the role of fire detection and alarm systems** in early warning and worker protection.
- **Implement effective employee accountability measures post-evacuation** to quickly confirm everyone is safe.
- **Recognize the necessity of leaving rescue operations to trained professionals** and establish procedures to protect untrained personnel.
